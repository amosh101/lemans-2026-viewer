2026 Regulations

How the 4 Le Mans classes work. LMH vs LMDh convergence. Balance of Performance. 2026-specific changes.

The headline story: The 2026 Le Mans Hypercar class uses two converging regulations — LMH (manufacturer-developed) and LMDh (customer chassis + spec hybrid). Both produce similar lap times, FIA balances them with BoP, and they're all classified together as "Hypercar" in the results.

Both classes are classified together as "Hypercar" in race results. BoP is the great equaliser. In 2026, the Hypercar class has 18 cars across 8 manufacturers: Toyota, Ferrari, Porsche (rumored), Cadillac, BMW, Alpine, Peugeot, Aston Martin, Genesis.

Balance of Performance (BoP)

BoP is the single most important technical factor in modern WEC. Before each round, the FIA publishes a table that adjusts each car's minimum weight, maximum power output, hybrid energy per lap, and fuel allocation to equalise lap times. Mid-race bulletins can adjust BoP if one car shows a clear pace advantage.

2026 Key Changes
Aero

New front splitter regulations for LMP2

Reduced downforce to address safety concerns at Tertre Rouge and Porsche Curves. 2-3 seconds per lap slower in fast corners, but reduces high-speed crash risk.

Hybrid

Increased hybrid deployment budget for LMDh

Up from 5 MJ/lap to 6 MJ/lap. Allows LMDh cars to better match LMH pace under low-fuel conditions. Major change in 2026 technical regulations.

Sporting

Reduced minimum driving time for Bronze-rated drivers in LMP2 Pro-Am

From 6 hours to 4 hours per Bronze driver. Makes the Pro-Am sub-class more accessible to gentleman drivers, helping the LMP2 field size.

Safety

New LED rain light system mandatory on all classes

High-visibility rain light integrated into rear bodywork. Visibility in spray at night is critical for the 24h race.
